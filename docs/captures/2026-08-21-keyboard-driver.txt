Blackroo Linux - the keyboard driver: typing on the PlayStation itself
======================================================================
Captured 2026-08-21 on the PAL SCPH-750x, build #68.

An Apple Bluetooth keyboard -> BlueRetro (multitap port B, SIO0 address 0x02)
-> drivers/char/psxkbd.c -> handle_scancode() -> the VT -> the GPU.

No host PC involved in the input path. The serial link only carries the log.

--- the driver finds it by itself ---

psxkbd: Lightspan keyboard driver, scanning SIO0 (both ports, multitap floors 01-04)
psxkbd: keyboard on port 1, address 02 (multitap floor)

The adapter is behind a multitap, so it answers 0x01+floor rather than 0x01.
Rather than be told where it is, the driver sweeps both ports and all four
floors at its idle rate and locks on. See GR-013.

--- the shell, driven from the keyboard ---

BLACKROO: /dev/console -> console "tty0" = tty device 4:1
brsh: console fd 0x00000004
brsh: userspace is alive on the PlayStation.

$ OI
brsh: unknown command: OI
$ PERFECT
brsh: unknown command: PERFECT
$ hello well
brsh: unknown command: hello well

Capitals mean the VT keymap is handling shift, so this is going through the
real console keyboard layer, not a private path.

"hello well" is the deliberate test, not a greeting: both doubled letters
survive. Before the de-duplication was removed this came through as "helo wel".

--- what the protocol turned out to be ---

Two hypotheses fitted the symptoms and wanted opposite fixes, so the driver was
made to dump raw frames and the question was settled with data. Over one
session of typing:

   nn=0:             244   nothing happened
   nn=1: 59          237   right shift MAKE
   nn=2: f0 59        26   right shift BREAK
   nn=1: 44 / f0 44   13   'o' down / up
   nn=1: 29 / f0 29   14   space down / up
   nn=1: 5a / f0 5a   14   enter down / up

Make and break codes, one event per frame, nn=0 when idle. An EVENT stream,
not a report of which keys are currently held.

That matters because the driver had been de-duplicating identical consecutive
frames, on the assumption that the adapter re-served its last report. It does
not. Two identical frames are two genuine keypresses, so the de-duplication was
eating real input - typing "hello" produced "helo".

The unbroken column of shift-makes that prompted the de-duplication in the
first place was ordinary typematic autorepeat: 237 makes against 26 releases is
a key being held. The console layer already handles that. What made it look
pathological was the debug printk, not the events.

Two further consequences of the trace:

  - The adapter sends at most one event per frame and does not queue, so an
    event between two polls is lost. The poll rate went from 50 Hz to 100 Hz to
    halve that window.
  - Idle frames outnumber busy ones even while typing (244 to 237), so a frame
    now stops as soon as nn says there is nothing to collect: five bytes
    instead of fifteen. That is what makes 100 Hz affordable.

--- the memory cards are unaffected ---

PSX joined card: driver initialized: 8 cards joined, total size = 381 Kbytes

Keyboard and cards share one SIO0 bus, told apart only by the address byte
(0x01+floor vs 0x81+floor). psx_sio0_trylock() in bu.c arbitrates; the keyboard
skips a sample rather than corrupt a card transfer, and bu_probing keeps it off
the bus entirely during the boot detection sweep.

--- also verified in this run ---

PSX: 1984 KB RAM configured (reg=0x8fbf0888)

The 2 MB config, after build.sh had silently been producing 8 MB kernels for a
2 MB console all session. See GR-015.
